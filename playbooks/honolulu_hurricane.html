How to read this playbook

Example row

Community (Census area)People in the risk areaMay need a public shelter (low – likely – high)
Kapolei
Census area 115		11,577232 – 347 – 579

Read it as: about 11,577 people live in the risk area of Kapolei. If this hazard hits, between 232 and 579 of them may need a public shelter; 347 is the likely number.

Census area

A neighborhood-sized area the U.S. Census uses, usually 1,200–8,000 people. The number (for example "Census area 308") is its Census ID. Each area is named after the Census place where most of its people live. "near" means no one in the area lives inside a named place; "rural near" means most people live outside named places.

Low – likely – high

The share of people in the risk area who may go to a public shelter: low 2%, likely 3%, high 5%. Where the rates come from: South Carolina Emergency Management Division hurricane shelter planning factors (2%, 3%, 5%).

Who has access and functional needs (CMIST)

  • C – Communication: people who speak English less than well (they may need interpreters and translated messages), and people with hearing or vision difficulty.
  • M – Medical: people who use power-dependent medical equipment (an estimate from HHS emPOWER).
  • I – Independence: people with a disability, people with cognitive difficulty (memory, decisions) and people age 65 and older.
  • S – Safety and support: people age 65 and older living alone, children and people living in group quarters (in the needs table).
  • T – Transportation: people in households without a car.

County total vs. one community

The county total counts every Census area in the county as hit at the same time, so it is an upper bound. Use the "Largest single community" table to plan for an event that hits one community.

Who else to plan for
  • Domestic-violence survivors: Served through confidential providers; no counts by design.
  • Off-grid households: No public Census-area data; county and health partners to supply.
  • People on dialysis or receiving home care: No public Census-area data; county and health partners to supply.
  • Earthquake: Not modeled; listed as a limitation in the plan.
  • Caregivers and personal care attendants: Stay with the person they support; count them for cots, meals and space.
  • Behavioral health: Mental-health staff at each shelter (ratio: section 8).
  • Niʻihau: Privately owned island, no public shelter; plan with the landowner and Kauaʻi County.
  • Families afraid to seek shelter (including immigrant families): The county publishes who may use shelters; trusted community messengers share it.

9. Results

  • Coverage: 436 of 436 Census areas have needs data (FEMA risk data joined for 435 of 436). Source: coverage check of the needs layer (CDC SVI 2022, FEMA NRI v1.20).
  • Backtest – Lahaina wildfire (2023-08-11): observed 1,418 people in shelters; model 254 / 635 / 1,423 (low / likely / high). This is a calibration event, not validation: the high rate was set from it. Source: Lahaina 2023 backtest (Maui Now count of people in shelters).
  • Time: a full rebuild from local data (14 steps, 20 scenarios, 20 playbooks) took 71.7 seconds on Apple M4 (2026-10-28).
